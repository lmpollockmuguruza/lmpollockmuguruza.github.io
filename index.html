Working papers

"Why Matching Platforms Go Unregulated: Traceability, Retrospective Voting and Platform Disclosure" (2026)

Abstract: Matching platforms in partner and labour markets earn revenue while users search, and therefore have weak incentives to disclose information that would shorten search. I ask why this inefficiency persists politically, despite intense public grievance about both markets. I embed a reduced-form platform in the canonical sanctioning model of retrospective voting. Because voters evaluate incumbents through realised market outcomes, the electoral return to mandating disclosure depends on the platform's effect relative to outcome noise, which I call the traceability ratio, and not on how poor outcomes are on average. When the platform can lobby, regulation occurs only for intermediate stakes: the electoral return to regulation is bounded by the value of office, while the platform's opposition grows with what disclosure would cost it, so the most consequential mandates are the least likely to pass. Preliminary and incomplete; comments welcome.

"Seeing Only the Winners: Selective Visibility, Congested Routes and Disappointment in Entry Markets" (2026)

Abstract: Graduates learn how hard it is to enter a career partly by watching the outcomes of others, and online those outcomes are selectively visible: offers are announced, rejections are not. I study the consequences in a simple model of an entry market with a scarce prestige route and an abundant core route. Applicants who take the visible share of successes at face value overestimate their chances of entering the prestige route. In the unique steady state, more selective visibility raises applications to the prestige route, lowers its acceptance rate, leaves more core vacancies unfilled, and widens the gap between how easy the route looks and how hard it is. A rise in the status of the prestige route also congests it, but applicants with accurate beliefs anticipate their rejections, so aggregate disappointment arises only from distorted beliefs, and status magnifies it. Informing some graduates of true acceptance rates raises the success rate of all applicants, so accurate information about entry routes is a public good. Preliminary and incomplete; comments welcome.

MSc coursework

"Corruption, Social Networks, and School Cheating" — MSc PSPE Dissertation (Department of Government, LSE, 2024-25)

Abstract: Do social networks moderate the effects of corruption on exam cheating? How do rural localities differ from urban localities in their response to corruption revelations? Using a novel dataset with locality-level family network measures for rural Mexico, I provide a compelling answer to these two questions, with additional data from Mexico's municipal audits and secondary school exam cheating rates. Using a staggered difference-in-differences identification strategy with modern estimators, I find that family networks moderate the effect of corruption revelations on exam cheating rates, with a positive, non-monotonic relationship between network strength and effect size. The analysis reveals that average degree (local connectivity) drives stronger effects than largest eigenvalue (global centrality), providing suggestive evidence that peer coordination rather than information cascades explains network moderation.

Prior

A workbench for research ideas — from the moment one occurs to you until it earns a real literature review, a real data request, or a real model. Capture takes one keystroke and no fields; the questions unfold only when you ask for them; and it versions your answers, so you can see how the thinking moved. It is named after the field it locks: what you thought the literature said before you read any of it, kept honest and comparable against what you later found. Identifying assumptions stay claims about institutions rather than properties of a file, and closing an idea is made to feel like the work it is. Local-first — no account, no server, no network calls at all.

On Exactitude in Science

Jorge Luis Borges, Collected Fictions, translated by Andrew Hurley

…In that Empire, the Art of Cartography attained such Perfection that the map of a single Province occupied the entirety of a City, and the map of the Empire, the entirety of a Province. In time, those Unconscionable Maps no longer satisfied, and the Cartographers Guilds struck a Map of the Empire whose size was that of the Empire, and which coincided point for point with it. The following Generations, who were not so fond of the Study of Cartography as their Forebears had been, saw that that vast Map was Useless, and not without some Pitilessness was it, that they delivered it up to the Inclemencies of Sun and Winters. In the Deserts of the West, still today, there are Tattered Ruins of that Map, inhabited by Animals and Beggars; in all the Land there is no other Relic of the Disciplines of Geography.

—Suárez Miranda, Viajes de varones prudentes, Libro IV, Cap. XLV, Lérida, 1658
